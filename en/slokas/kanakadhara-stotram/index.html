🌸 Kanakadhara Stotram

A young Adi Shankaracharya begged for alms at the door of a woman so poor she had nothing to give. She searched her empty house and found one dried gooseberry — and gave him that.

Moved, he sang this hymn to Lakshmi. It is said a rain of golden fruit fell into her courtyard.

Kanakadhara = the stream of gold.

📖 Verses

The Story Verses Meaning

How This Hymn Was Born

1
Adi Shankaracharya, as a young boy, went begging for alms.
Meaning: He came to the house of a woman so poor she had nothing to give. Ashamed, she searched her empty house — and found a single dried amla (gooseberry). She gave him that.
Note: She gave the only thing she had.

Moved beyond words, the boy Shankara stood at her door and sang a hymn to Lakshmi — and it is said that a rain of golden amla fell into her courtyard.

That hymn is the Kanakadhara Stotram — 'the stream of gold'.

Selected Verses

2
Angam hareh pulaka-bhooshanam aashrayantee |
Bhringaanganeva mukulaabharanam tamaalam |
Angeekritaakhila-vibhootir apaanga-leelaa |
Maangalya-daastu mama mangala-devataayaah ||
Meaning: She rests upon the form of Hari, adorning it as a bee settles on a blossoming tree. May the playful sidelong glance of the Goddess of auspiciousness — she who holds all prosperity — bring blessing upon me.
Note: The famous opening verse. Note: Shankara does not ask for gold. He asks only for her apaanga — her glance.
3
Sarasija-nilaye saroja-haste |
Dhavala-tamaamshuka-gandha-maalya-shobhe |
Bhagavati harivallabhe manojne |
Tribhuvana-bhooti-kari praseeda mahyam ||
Meaning: O Goddess seated on the lotus, holding a lotus, radiant in white garments, fragrance and garlands; beloved of Hari; enchanting; bringer of prosperity to the three worlds — be gracious to me.

Why This Hymn Matters

4
What Kanakadhara really teaches
Meaning: The woman had nothing. She gave a single dried fruit — and it was enough to move a saint to song, and the Goddess to grace.
Note: The hymn is not about getting rich.

It is about a poor woman who gave anyway — and a young monk who saw that, and was moved.

Wealth came to her not because she asked, but because she gave.

That is the whole teaching.

Recited especially on Fridays, and during Varalakshmi Vratam.
